# Business-writing benchmarks

Use these writers as standards for particular qualities, not as voices to
imitate or evidence that a model matches their literary achievement. This is
a selected English-prose reference set, not a ranking of the greatest writers.
The business task determines which qualities matter.

## George Orwell: meaning before a verbal formula

In [Politics and the English Language](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/),
read the discussion immediately before the six rules. Orwell argues for
choosing words to express a clear thought, rejects fake simplicity, and warns
that inherited phrases can change the meaning. His own sixth rule makes
judgment more important than mechanical obedience.

Benchmark: Can the reader state the actual claim after one reading? Are
concrete actions and evidence limits visible, or has polished phrasing
concealed them? Prefer the sentence that conveys the right meaning, even if
it needs a technical term or an extra clause.

## Mark Twain: exact words and necessary detail

In [Fenimore Cooper's Literary Offences](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3172/pg3172-images.html),
rules 12–18 include “Use the right word, not its second cousin.” They also
include “Not omit necessary details.” Cutting words and retaining essential
information are complementary duties.

Benchmark: Does each word mean exactly what the evidence supports? Has an
edit removed a material distinction, qualification, or step? Transfer that
precision to business writing, not Twain's satirical aggression.

## Abraham Lincoln: judgment, limits, and a useful request

Read the [letter to General Grant of April 30, 1864](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/14721/14721-h/14721-h.htm)
in *Speeches and Letters of Abraham Lincoln, 1832–1865*, under that dated
heading. Lincoln expresses confidence, states the
limits of his knowledge and intervention, and invites Grant to identify help
Lincoln can provide. The letter is a practical communication, not a speech.

Benchmark: Are the writer's knowledge, responsibility, and request distinct?
Does courtesy serve the relationship without obscuring the point? A useful
qualification is part of the judgment, not clutter to delete.

## Winston Churchill: concise decisions with supporting depth

Read the [Brevity memorandum of August 9, 1940](https://clearglobal.ca/brevity.html),
a transcription of the War Cabinet memo identified as Churchill Archives
Centre CHAR 23/5. Churchill asks for main points in short paragraphs, detailed
analysis in an appendix when needed, and the removal of official padding.
This is a primary text reproduced by a plain-language organization, not a
claim that the hosting site is the archive.

Benchmark: Can a busy reader find the decision and its reasons quickly?
Is supporting detail accessible without occupying the opening? Does the
summary retain the qualifications required to use that detail honestly?

## How to compare drafts

Use realistic business prompts with facts and audience specified. Include
brief answers, a consequential recommendation, a customer message, an edit,
and a detailed handoff. Keep the writer model and task the same across
conditions. Give each generation a fresh context. Do not give the writer
the expected answer or the evaluator's checklist.

First check factual fidelity and completion of the request. A false claim,
material omission, invented commitment, or violated explicit requirement
cannot be redeemed by elegance. Then compare first-reading clarity, economy,
organization, appropriate detail, and tone. Brevity means no wasted words,
not the lowest word count. Use ties when the difference is immaterial.

For an independent comparison, randomize labels and hide the instruction
condition from the judge. Retain prompts and outputs, report failures as well
as preferences, and distinguish a small sample from a general performance
claim. Include fresh tasks after a revision so the examples do not become
the test. Record which old instruction was replaced and what behavior the
comparison actually observed; do not infer improvement from a shorter skill.
