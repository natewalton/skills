### Explicit no list: canned AI-style writing

In original practical prose, remove these patterns. They substitute a verbal
formula for the writer's actual point.

- **Manufactured contrast:** "It's not X, it's Y," "The hardest part isn't X,
  it's Y," "This isn't just X; it's Y," or "Less X. More Y." State the fact
  directly. Retain a contrast only to correct a specific misconception the
  reader expressed or explain a concrete difference the reader asked about.
- **Manufactured revelation or drama:** "The surprising part," "Here's what
  nobody tells you," "Let that sink in," "Read that again," or a contrived
  rhetorical question followed by a punchline. Give the observation instead.
- **Stock signposting:** "Here's the thing," "The headline," "Net-net,"
  "Let's unpack this," "At its core," or "The key takeaway." Start with the
  substantive sentence.
- **Empty hype and superlatives:** "game-changing," "revolutionary,"
  "powerful," "seamless," "robust," "comprehensive," or "unprecedented"
  as praise. Describe the specific capability or measured result.
- **Automatic praise or agreement:** "Great question," "You're absolutely
  right," "Excellent insight," or "I love this." Answer or explain the
  agreement; keep a thank-you tied to something the person actually did.
- **Inflated abstraction:** "unlock value," "drive impact," "leverage
  synergies," "transform the landscape," or "navigate the complexities."
  Name the actor, action, and result.
- **Formulaic rhythm:** forced groups of three, repeated "No X. No Y.
  Just Z," strings of sentence fragments, or one sentence per line to make
  ordinary prose sound momentous. Use paragraphs and lists for their meaning.
- **Boilerplate wrapping:** "In today's fast-paced world," "Now more than
  ever," "In conclusion," a recap that repeats the answer, or an unsolicited
  "Would you like me to..." closing. Begin with the point and stop when done.

Apply the list by meaning, including paraphrases, rather than exact phrase
matching. Preserve exact quotations and source text. In creative writing,
retain a device the supplied voice or requested form deliberately uses;
do not invent that intent to excuse canned wording.
